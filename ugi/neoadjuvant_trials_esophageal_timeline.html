1992
RTOG 85-01
JAMA 1999 (long-term) · Herskovic et al.
Definitive CRT SCC + Adeno
First randomized trial proving chemoradiation is superior to radiation alone for esophageal cancer. Established CRT as a curative non-surgical option.

Design
Phase III randomized trial. CRT (5-FU 1000 mg/m² d1-4 + cisplatin 75 mg/m² d1 with concurrent 50 Gy) vs RT alone (64 Gy). Patients with cT1-3, N0-1, M0 esophageal SCC or adenocarcinoma.
Population
121 patients. Predominantly squamous cell carcinoma. Non-surgical candidates or patients with locoregional disease amenable to definitive treatment.
Key outcomes
Median survival 14 vs 9 months (CRT vs RT). 5-year OS 27% vs 0%. Projected 8-year and 10-year survival rates of 22% and 20% respectively for CRT. Local failure reduced from 65% to 47%.
Toxicity
Higher acute toxicity with CRT including mucositis and myelosuppression. However, long-term survivors had acceptable quality of life. Treatment-related mortality was comparable between arms.
Clinical impact: This trial eliminated RT alone as a curative option and made concurrent CRT the standard for non-surgical esophageal cancer management. Still referenced in NCCN guidelines as the foundation for definitive CRT.

Design
Single-center randomized trial. Neoadjuvant CRT (5-FU + cisplatin + 40 Gy in 15 fractions) followed by surgery vs surgery alone.
Population
113 patients with adenocarcinoma of the esophagus. All patients were considered surgically resectable at enrollment.
Key outcomes
Median survival 16 vs 11 months. 3-year OS 32% vs 6% favoring trimodality. pCR rate of 25% in the CRT arm.
Limitations
Small sample size. Single-center design. Criticized for unusually poor outcomes in the surgery-alone arm. Non-standard RT fractionation.
Clinical impact: Despite its limitations, this was one of the first trials to demonstrate the trimodality concept (CRT + surgery) for esophageal adenocarcinoma, helping pave the way for larger confirmatory studies.

Landmark trial establishing perioperative ECF chemotherapy as standard of care for gastric and GEJ adenocarcinoma versus surgery alone.

Design
Phase III multicenter RCT. 3 pre-op + 3 post-op cycles of ECF (epirubicin 50 mg/m², cisplatin 60 mg/m², 5-FU 200 mg/m² CI) vs surgery alone. Primary endpoint: overall survival.
Population
503 patients with stage II+ gastric, GEJ, or lower esophageal adenocarcinoma. 26% had GEJ tumors, 11% lower esophageal. Predominantly male.
Key outcomes
5-year OS 36.3% vs 23.0%. HR for death 0.75 (95% CI 0.60-0.93; p=0.009). Improved PFS (HR 0.66). Tumors were significantly smaller and less advanced at resection in perioperative chemo group.
Completion & toxicity
86% started pre-op chemo, but only 42% completed all 6 planned cycles. G3/4 neutropenia 29%. Post-operative complications and mortality similar between groups.
Clinical impact: The first trial to demonstrate that perioperative chemotherapy improves survival in gastroesophageal cancer. Became the global standard for over a decade until superseded by FLOT4. Still used in some centers for patients unable to tolerate FLOT.

Phase III French multicenter trial. 2-3 pre-op + 3-4 post-op cycles of 5-FU + cisplatin vs surgery alone. 75% had lower esophageal or GEJ adenocarcinoma.
Key outcomes
5-year OS 38% vs 24% (p=0.02). 5-year DFS 34% vs 19%. Significant improvement in curative (R0) resection rate. Prematurely terminated due to low accrual.

Design
Phase III. Neoadjuvant CRT (5-FU + cisplatin + 50.4 Gy) followed by surgery vs surgery alone. Stages I-III esophageal cancer. Closed early due to poor accrual (56 of planned 475 patients).
Key outcomes
Median OS 4.5 vs 1.8 years (p=0.002). 5-year OS 39% vs 16%. Median follow-up 6 years. Strong survival benefit despite very small sample.
Clinical impact: Although severely underpowered, this is the only North American phase III trial supporting neoadjuvant CRT for esophageal cancer and is referenced in NCCN guidelines as category 1 evidence for 5-FU/cisplatin-based preoperative CRT.
Defining the standard of care (2012–2019)
2012
CROSS
NEJM 2012; Lancet Oncol 2015; JCO 2021 (10-yr) · van Hagen, Shapiro, Eyck et al.
Neoadjuvant CRT SCC + Adeno Practice-changing
The defining trial of modern esophageal cancer management. Weekly carboplatin and paclitaxel with 41.4 Gy followed by surgery became the global standard of care. Benefits persist at 10 years.

Design
Phase III, multicenter RCT (8 Dutch centers). Neoadjuvant CRT (carboplatin AUC 2 + paclitaxel 50 mg/m² weekly × 5 cycles + 41.4 Gy/23 fr) followed by surgery vs surgery alone. cT1N1 or cT2-3N0-1M0.
Population
368 patients (178 CRT + surgery, 188 surgery alone). 75% adenocarcinoma, 25% SCC. 58% distal esophagus, 24% GEJ, 18% mid/upper esophagus. Median age 60 years.
Surgical details
~45% transhiatal approach. Median 15-18 lymph nodes removed. Two-field lymphadenectomy for tumors proximal to carina. R0 resection 92% vs 69% (p<0.001).
Tolerability
95% completed full neoadjuvant therapy. Only 8% G3-4 hematologic toxicity. Post-operative complications similar between arms. No adverse impact on long-term quality of life.

Design
Phase II/III, multicenter RCT (38 German centers). 4 pre-op + 4 post-op cycles of FLOT (docetaxel 50 mg/m², oxaliplatin 85 mg/m², leucovorin 200 mg/m², 5-FU 2600 mg/m² 24h CI) q2w vs 3+3 cycles ECF/ECX.
Population
716 patients. Resectable gastric or GEJ adenocarcinoma (≥cT2 and/or cN+). 56% GEJ, 44% gastric. Adenocarcinoma only. Median age 62. 74% male.
Key outcomes
Median OS 50 vs 35 months (HR 0.77; p=0.012). 3-year OS 57% vs 48%. PFS 30 vs 18 months (HR 0.75; p=0.004). pCR 16% vs 6% (p=0.02). R0 resection 84% vs 77% (p=0.011).
Tolerability
90% completed pre-op chemo, but only 50% completed post-op cycles. More G3/4 neutropenia with FLOT. Less nausea/vomiting. Serious AE rate 27% in both arms. Pronounced benefit in Siewert type I (HR 0.60).
Clinical impact: Replaced ECF/ECX as the perioperative standard. NCCN recommends FLOT for select patients with good performance status due to considerable toxicity; FOLFOX is the preferred perioperative option for most patients. Set the stage for the ESOPEC head-to-head comparison with CROSS.

CheckMate 577
NEJM 2021 · Kelly et al.
Adjuvant IO SCC + Adeno Practice-changing
Adjuvant nivolumab for 1 year after neoadjuvant CRT plus surgery in patients without complete pathologic response. Doubled median disease-free survival and changed the trimodality paradigm.

Design
Phase III, global, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled. Adjuvant nivolumab 240 mg IV q2w × 16 weeks then 480 mg q4w to complete 1 year vs placebo. Resected stage II-III esophageal/GEJ cancer with residual pathologic disease after neoadjuvant CRT.
Population
794 patients (532 nivo, 262 placebo). 71% adenocarcinoma, 29% SCC. All had R0 resection after neoadjuvant CRT but residual pathologic disease (non-pCR). Median age ~62.
Outcomes by histology
Adenocarcinoma: median DFS 19.4 vs 11.1 months (HR 0.75). SCC: median DFS 29.7 vs 11.0 months (HR 0.49). Particularly strong benefit in SCC, with hazard ratio nearly halved.
Tolerability
G3/4 treatment-related AE in 13% (nivo) vs 6% (placebo). Most common immune-related AEs: rash, pruritus, diarrhea, fatigue. No new safety signals. Manageable with standard IO toxicity protocols.
Clinical impact: Transformed the trimodality approach by adding a fourth component (immunotherapy) for patients without pCR. Now standard of care per NCCN and KHCC guidelines after CROSS + surgery. Referenced in the KHCC CPG as nivolumab 240 mg q2w for 1 year. Notably, ESOPEC did not incorporate this, which is a key criticism of that trial.

CROSS vs Chemo Adenocarcinoma
First head-to-head comparison of CROSS versus perioperative chemotherapy (modified MAGIC/FLOT). No survival difference found — establishing non-inferiority of perioperative chemo.

Design
Phase III, open-label, international. CROSS (neoadjuvant CRT + surgery) vs perioperative chemotherapy (modified MAGIC before 2018, then FLOT) + surgery. Esophageal/GEJ adenocarcinoma.
Population
377 patients (70% of target — closed early). Median follow-up 24.5 months. 143 deaths (70 CROSS, 73 chemo). Locoregional esophageal/GEJ adenocarcinoma.
Key outcomes
3-year OS: 56% (CROSS) vs 57% (chemo). HR 1.02. No survival difference. Median OS 49.2 vs 48.0 months. Pathologic endpoints (pCR 12% vs 5%, R0 96% vs 82%) favored CROSS arm.
Limitations
Closed early at 70% accrual — underpowered. Mixed chemotherapy regimens (MAGIC then switched to FLOT). Only 15% received FLOT. Median follow-up relatively short at 24.5 months.
Clinical impact: Strongly suggested non-inferiority of perioperative chemotherapy to neoadjuvant CRT, making both viable options. However, the mixed chemo regimens and early closure limited definitive conclusions. Set the stage for the more definitive ESOPEC comparison.

Design
Phase III, prospective, multicenter RCT (25 German centers). FLOT (4 pre-op + 4 post-op cycles) vs CROSS (41.4 Gy + weekly carbo/pacli × 5 then surgery). Esophageal adenocarcinoma only (cT1N+ or cT2-4a N0/+ M0). Enrolled Feb 2016 – Apr 2020.
Population
438 patients (221 FLOT, 217 CROSS). 89% male. Median age 63. cT3/4 in 80.5%. cN+ in 79.7%. Baseline characteristics well-balanced. Pure adenocarcinoma — no SCC.
Treatment completion
Neoadjuvant started in >90% both arms. Full RT given in 98%. 75% received all 5 chemo cycles in CROSS. FLOT: 87.3% completed neoadjuvant. Surgery rates: 86.4% (FLOT) vs 82.9% (CROSS).
Safety
G3+ AE: 58% (FLOT) vs 50% (CROSS). Serious AE: 47.3% vs 41.8%. Postoperative morbidity comparable (Clavien-Dindo). 30-day mortality: 1.0% vs 1.7%. 90-day mortality: 3.2% vs 5.6% (NS).

Controversies & limitations
CROSS arm underperformance: The pCR rate of 10% in the CROSS arm was lower than expected (23% in the original CROSS trial; ~20% in real-world Dutch registry data). This raised concerns about whether the CROSS regimen was optimally delivered.

No adjuvant nivolumab: ESOPEC was designed before CheckMate 577 results. Today, non-pCR patients receiving CROSS would get adjuvant nivolumab — making the true contemporary comparison CROSS + nivo vs FLOT, which was not tested.

Radiation quality concerns: Critics noted potentially outdated RT techniques and questioned whether modern IMRT/VMAT delivery might have improved CROSS outcomes.

SCC excluded: FLOT has never been studied in squamous cell carcinoma. CROSS remains the clear standard for SCC (5-year OS 60% in the original CROSS trial).

Post-op FLOT completion: Only about 50% of patients historically complete all 4 post-operative FLOT cycles, raising questions about the feasibility of the full perioperative approach.
Clinical impact: ESOPEC supports perioperative FLOT as the preferred strategy for fit patients with resectable esophageal adenocarcinoma. However, the debate continues — the optimal strategy may be CROSS + adjuvant nivolumab for non-pCR patients, or even combinations of FLOT + CRT. For SCC, CROSS remains unchallenged. Practice is evolving rapidly with immunotherapy integration.
