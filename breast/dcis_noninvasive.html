Risk Factors — Identical to Invasive BC
  • Older age, proliferative breast disease, increased breast density
  • Nulliparity, older age at first birth, early menarche, late menopause
  • Long-term postmenopausal HRT, elevated BMI (postmenopausal)
  • BRCA1/2 mutations — rates similar to invasive BC
  • First-degree family history 10–35%
📌
DCIS is classified as Tis, Stage 0. DCIS with microinvasion (≤1 mm foci) = T1mi → upstaged to Stage I.

Diagnosis & Imaging

Section 02
📌
MCQ PEARL: ~90% of DCIS presents as microcalcifications on mammography — NOT as a palpable mass or bloody nipple discharge. Before screening era: palpable mass was the main presentation.
Mammographic Features
  • Microcalcifications: 80–90% of mammographic DCIS presentations
  • Linear branching calcifications → high-grade comedo DCIS
  • Fine granular calcifications → lower-grade micropapillary/cribriform
  • Mammography underestimates extent in 44% of micropapillary DCIS (vs. 12% comedo)
  • Magnification views reduce underestimation to 14% — use routinely
  • Bilateral diagnostic mammography required — 0.5–3% have contralateral synchronous disease
MRI — Role & Evidence
  • Sensitivity up to 98% for high-grade DCIS (NCCN 2026)
  • MRI detected multicentric disease in 4.3% of DCIS (Hollingsworth)
  • Contralateral DCIS: sensitivity 71%, specificity 90% (Lehman)
  • MSK study (2,300 pts): NO reduction in locoregional recurrence with preop MRI
  • NCCN: MRI should NOT solely drive mastectomy decision
  • HER2 status in DCIS does NOT alter management (NCCN 2026)
Diagnostic Biopsy — Core Needle is Standard
Core Biopsy FindingUpgrade Rate on ExcisionAction
DCIS on core biopsy20% invasive on excisionProceed to definitive surgery
ADH25–30% DCIS/invasiveExcisional biopsy (usually)
Radial scarUp to 20%Consider excision
Flat epithelial atypia (FEA)5–10%Excision if calcs not all removed; observe if fully sampled
🚫
Fine-needle aspiration CANNOT distinguish DCIS from invasive disease. Core needle biopsy is the diagnostic standard. Excisional biopsy reserved for inconclusive/discordant results.

Surgical Treatment

Section 03
🏆
Key Principle: No survival advantage has ever been demonstrated for mastectomy over BCS + radiation in DCIS. Mastectomy LRR ≈ 2.6% vs. BCS + RT ≈ 13.6% at 10 years (Stuart 2015 meta-analysis, 9,404 patients). Disease-specific survival is equivalent.
Margin Guidelines — SSO / ASTRO / ASCO Joint Consensus 2016

Meta-analysis of 20 studies, 7,883 patients, 865 IBTRs, median follow-up 78.3 months.

Margin WidthStatusRecommendation
≥ 2 mmADEQUATEStandard for BCS + WBI; 2× lower IBTR vs. positive margins
< 1 mmINADEQUATERe-excision required
1–2 mmJUDGMENTClinical judgment — consider grade, age, RT planned
Positive marginCONCERN2× increased IBTR risk vs. negative margins
Chest wall / skin boundaryEXCEPTIONRT boost to scar = acceptable alternative to re-excision
BCS — Candidate Criteria
  • Unicentric disease
  • Tumor-to-breast size ratio allows acceptable cosmesis
  • No evidence of diffuse microcalcifications on mammography
  • No contraindications to radiotherapy
  • Margins can be achieved at ≥ 2 mm
Mastectomy Indications
  • Diffuse malignant-appearing calcifications throughout breast
  • Persistent positive margins after re-excision
  • Contraindication to RT (e.g., scleroderma, pregnancy, prior RT)
  • Large lesion (>4 cm) with unfavorable breast-to-tumor ratio
  • Multicentric disease impairing cosmesis
  • Patient preference (counsel: no survival benefit)
  • DCIS recurrence after prior BCS + RT
⚠️
Scleroderma MCQ Trap: Scleroderma = relative contraindication to radiation → skin-sparing mastectomy + SLN dissection at same operation. Cannot map lymphatics later after mastectomy if invasive disease found on final pathology.
☢️

Radiation Therapy — Five Landmark RCTs

Section 04
📊
Universal finding across all 5 trials: RT reduces IBTR (both invasive AND noninvasive) by ~50% relative reduction. NONE demonstrate an overall survival benefit. RT reduces recurrence regardless of age, grade, necrosis, or margin status.
NSABP B-17
NSABP B-17
818 women · BCS ± RT · 1985–1990
818 women with localized DCIS randomized after margin-negative resection to BCS or BCS + RT.
15-Year Results Noninvasive IBTR: 15.7% → 8.8% (HR 0.53, CI 0.35–0.80)
Invasive IBTR: 19.4% → 8.9% (HR 0.48, CI 0.33–0.69)
Overall survival: No difference
~50% reduction No OS benefit
EORTC 10853
EORTC 10853
1,010 women · 1986–1996
1,010 women randomized to BCS ± RT. Confirmed B-17 findings at longer follow-up.
15-Year Results Noninvasive recurrence: 16% → 8% (HR 0.49, CI 0.33–0.73)
Invasive recurrence: 16% → 10% (HR 0.61, CI 0.42–0.87)
Overall survival: No difference
Confirms B-17 No OS benefit
UK/ANZ DCIS
UK / ANZ DCIS Trial
1,701 women · 3 arms · 1990–1998
3 arms: no adjuvant, RT or tamoxifen, or RT + tamoxifen. Enrolled from UK, Australia, New Zealand.
12.7-Year Follow-up Ipsilateral DCIS: HR 0.38 (CI 0.22–0.63)
Ipsilateral invasive: HR 0.32 (CI 0.19–0.56)
Tamoxifen reduced all new events: HR 0.71
Durable RT benefit Tamoxifen additive
RTOG 9804
RTOG 9804
636 pts · Low/intermediate grade · ≤25 mm · margins ≥3 mm
Randomized "good-risk" DCIS to RT vs. observation ± tamoxifen. Closed early (636 of 1,790 planned).
15-Year Results IBTR: 7.1% (RT) vs. 15.1% (obs), p < 0.001
Invasive local recurrence: 5.6% vs. 9.5%, p = 0.03
Even low-grade benefits from RT
SweDCIS
SweDCIS Trial
1,046 women · 1987–1999
Swedish trial randomizing women after lumpectomy to RT vs. observation.
20-Year Follow-up Local recurrence reduction: 37.5%
Breast cancer-specific survival: No difference
Overall survival: No difference
Longest follow-up No OS benefit
NSABP B-39
NSABP B-39 / RTOG 0413 — APBI
4,216 pts · APBI vs. WBI · 2005–2013
APBI vs. WBI in DCIS (518 vs. 513 pts) and early invasive BC. APBI = 4–5 days vs. 4–6 weeks WBI.
Primary Endpoint IBTR: 4% (APBI) vs. 3% (WBI) — HR 1.22 (90% CI 0.94–1.58)
DCIS subgroup: 6.2% vs. 5.8% (HR 1.01, NS)
APBI did NOT meet equivalence to WBI
APBI not equivalent No OS difference

NSABP B-17 reanalysis: ALL patient subgroups benefited from RT regardless of clinical or mammographic characteristics. Wong (Harvard): 13% local recurrence at 11 years even in favorable DCIS without RT. RT omission remains controversial even in "good-risk" DCIS.

Active Surveillance Trials

Section 05
📡
Context: Much of the DCIS incidence rise is likely overdetection of biologically indolent lesions. SEER data (Ryser et al.): 10-yr invasive risk without any treatment = 12.2% (low grade) to 17.6% (high grade). Three trials are investigating whether surgery can be safely omitted in low-risk DCIS.
COMET
COMET Trial
USA · RCT · Federal funding 2016
Comparing an Operation to Monitoring, With or Without Endocrine Therapy. Standard care (surgery ± RT) vs. active surveillance. Both arms may choose endocrine therapy.
Inclusion Criteria (HIGH-YIELD) Age ≥ 40 · Grade 1 or 2 (comedonecrosis OK) · ER+ and/or PR+ · No invasion/microinvasion · Uni/bilateral, unifocal/multifocal/multicentric
Primary Endpoint Ipsilateral invasive breast cancer rate
LORD
LORD Trial
Europe (EORTC) · Noninferiority · Jan 2017
LOw Risk DCIS — multicenter international noninferiority trial. Active surveillance (annual mammogram only, no locoregional/endocrine therapy) vs. standard surgical treatment ± endocrine.
Inclusion Low-grade DCIS only
Primary Endpoint Ipsilateral invasive BC at 10 years
LORIS
LORIS Trial
UK · Phase III · Accrual complete March 2020
Low Risk DCIS — UK phase III comparing active surveillance vs. surgery for low/intermediate grade DCIS. Accrual completed March 2020; results still pending.
Inclusion Low/intermediate grade · No high-grade features
Primary Endpoint Invasive ipsilateral event-free survival
LORETTA
LORETTA Trial
Japan · Phase II · Ongoing
Active surveillance + aromatase inhibitor for low/intermediate grade ER+ DCIS in postmenopausal women.
Inclusion Low/intermediate grade · ER+ · Postmenopausal
Primary Endpoint 2-year invasive BC rate

Endocrine Therapy

Section 06
📌
Key Rule: Endocrine therapy should ONLY be used in ER+ DCIS. No benefit demonstrated in ER− disease (NSABP B-24 subgroup analysis).
B-24
NSABP B-24
1,804 women · BCS + RT · Tamoxifen vs. placebo × 5 yrs
16% had positive resection margins. Tamoxifen 20 mg/day vs. placebo for 5 years after BCS + RT.
7-Year Results Breast events: 10.0% (tam) vs. 16.9% (placebo)
Ipsilateral invasive: 2.6% vs. 5.3%
Contralateral neoplasms: 2.3% vs. 4.9%
OS: No difference (95% both groups)
ER+ subgroup: >50% RR reduction at 10 yrs
B-35
NSABP B-35
3,000+ postmenopausal · Anastrozole vs. tamoxifen × 5 yrs
Postmenopausal DCIS patients after BCS + RT, 333 US/Canadian centers, 2003–2006.
9-Year Follow-up Anastrozole superior: HR 0.73 (CI 0.56–0.96)
Benefit only in women < 60 years
Venous thromboembolic events higher with tamoxifen
IBIS-II
IBIS-II
Postmenopausal · Anastrozole 1 mg vs. placebo × 5 yrs · 18 countries
Women aged 40–70 from 18 countries, 2003–2012. Anastrozole vs. placebo for 5 years.
5-Year Results Breast cancer: 2% (anastrozole) vs. 4% (placebo)
HR 0.47 (CI 0.32–0.68) — >50% risk reduction
Drug Selection by Patient Profile
Menopausal StatusPreferred AgentKey Toxicities / Rationale
Premenopausal · ER+ Tamoxifen 20 mg × 5 yrs DVT/PE, endometrial cancer (2–7×), stroke, cataracts; do NOT use AIs
Postmenopausal · intact uterus · osteopenia Raloxifene 60 mg × 5 yrs Equivalent to tamoxifen (NSABP P-2); fewer endometrial/thromboembolic events; helps bone density
Postmenopausal · no uterine concern Anastrozole 1 mg or Exemestane 25 mg × 5 yrs Osteoporosis, arthralgias; fewer CV events vs. tamoxifen
ER− DCIS · any NO endocrine therapy No benefit demonstrated
ℹ️
ASCO Risk Reduction Threshold: Pharmacologic risk reduction recommended for 5-year absolute risk ≥ 1.7%. ALL patients with ADH, ALH, or LCIS automatically meet this criterion.

Sentinel Lymph Node Biopsy in DCIS

Section 07
📌
MCQ Rule: SLNB is NOT routinely recommended for DCIS undergoing BCS. It IS indicated when mastectomy is planned, because lymphatic mapping becomes impossible after mastectomy if invasive disease is found on final pathology.
SLN Indications in DCIS

Mastectomy for DCIS (any size)Cannot map after mastectomy if invasion foundREQUIRED
High-grade DCISHigher occult invasion riskRECOMMENDED
Palpable DCIS / >4 cmHigh risk of coexistent invasionRECOMMENDED

📊
MD Anderson Data (Francis et al., 1,234 pts): Positive SLN in 10.7%. Upstaging in 26.5%. At 61.7-month FU: NO difference in disease-free survival between DCIS with vs. without positive SLN. High isolated tumor cells likely = benign mechanical transport.
✅
Delayed SLN after BCS: Possible as a second procedure if invasive disease found on final pathology — lymphatic channels are preserved after lumpectomy. NOT possible after mastectomy.

LCIS — Key Facts
  • NOT a premalignant lesion — it is a bilateral risk MARKER
  • Risk: ~1%/year; ~20–30% at 15 years
  • Mean age: 44–46 yrs (10–15 yrs younger than invasive BC)
  • Often bilateral, multicentric, multifocal (contralateral in 30–90%)
  • E-cadherin: NEGATIVE in LCIS (positive in DCIS) — key differentiator
  • Time to invasive cancer: 10–15 years (not 5 — MCQ trap!)
  • Most common presentation: incidental finding on biopsy for another lesion
ADH — Key Facts
  • Lies between usual ductal hyperplasia and low-grade DCIS
  • Defined: < 2 terminal ductal-lobular units, area < 2 mm
  • 4–5× increased lifetime risk of breast cancer
  • 25–30% upgrade to DCIS/invasive on surgical excision
  • 25-year cumulative incidence of BC: 30%
  • Strongly ER+ and PR+, rarely HER2/neu-amplified
⚠️
ADH upgrade risk factors: ≥3 TDLUs involved + significant atypia + necrosis + <95% calcifications removed → excisional biopsy indicated.

MCQ Practice Bank

Section 11
Sources: MCQs in General Surgical Oncology (Springer 2024) · DeVita Principles & Practice · MD Anderson Case Scenarios
🟢 Springer MCQs — DCIS & LCIS Questions
Q9. A 58-year-old postmenopausal woman with DCIS. Which statement is INCORRECT? Option C: "The average age of diagnosis is between 30 and 35 years."
✗ C — INCORRECT statement
The average age of diagnosis for DCIS is 54–56 years, not 30–35. DCIS occurs primarily in postmenopausal women.
Q11. Which statement about DCIS is INCORRECT? Option A: "Mostly presents as a palpable mass or bloody nipple discharge."
✗ A — INCORRECT statement
About 90% of DCIS is identified by microcalcifications on mammography. Palpable mass was the pre-screening era presentation.
Q7 (LCIS). Which statement is INCORRECT? Option B: "The time to develop invasive cancer is about 5 years."
✗ B — INCORRECT statement
The time to develop invasive cancer from LCIS is 10–15 years, not 5 years.
Q13. Which LCIS statement is INCORRECT? Option A: "Is a premalignant lesion."
✗ A — INCORRECT statement
LCIS is NOT premalignant — it is a bilateral risk marker (~1%/year risk). It does not necessarily progress to invasive cancer.
🟢 DeVita MCQs — DCIS Chapter
Q7.12. Which statement about DCIS is TRUE? A: DCIS accounts for 15–30% of mammographically detected cancers.
✓ A — TRUE
DCIS = 15–30% of mammographically detected cancers. Most common in women aged 49–69. Younger women have HIGHER (not lower) local recurrence risk. Routine SLN biopsy NOT recommended in BCS for nonpalpable DCIS (axillary involvement only 1–2%).
Q7.13. RT after lumpectomy for DCIS. Which is TRUE? D: None of the above.
✓ D — None of the above
RT reduces BOTH invasive AND noninvasive IBTR after BCS for DCIS. It does NOT improve overall survival. Tamoxifen does not negate the benefit of RT.
Q7.16. Which is an absolute contraindication to BCS requiring RT? D: All of the above.
✓ D — All of the above
Absolute contraindications: RT during pregnancy; diffuse suspicious/malignant microcalcifications; widespread disease not resectable through single incision with negative margins + good cosmesis.
🟠 MD Anderson Case Scenario MCQs
Case 1 Q3. Which patient is appropriate for COMET Trial? D: 42-year-old female, intermediate-grade DCIS with comedonecrosis, ER+.
✓ D — Correct
COMET includes grade 1 or 2 REGARDLESS of comedonecrosis, ER+, no invasion, age ≥40. Options A (invasive), B (ipsilateral recurrence), and C (microinvasion) all EXCLUDE from COMET.
Case 1 Q4. 52-year-old with scleroderma, high-grade DCIS, comedonecrosis, ER−. Appropriate management?
✓ D — Skin-sparing mastectomy + SLN dissection
Scleroderma = relative contraindication to radiation → BCS not appropriate. SLN at mastectomy because lymphatic mapping impossible later if invasive found on final path.
Case 2 Q1. After core biopsy, which pathologic result warrants excisional biopsy? B: Pleomorphic LCIS.
✓ B — PLCIS
Pleomorphic LCIS has higher progression rate → excision with negative margins required. ALH, flat epithelial atypia, and florid ductal hyperplasia do NOT typically require excision unless discordant with imaging.
Case 2 Q2. 58-year-old postmenopausal, intact uterus, osteopenia, LCIS on biopsy. Recommended medication?
✓ D — Raloxifene
Tamoxifen: higher uterine cancer risk in postmenopausal women with intact uterus. AIs: higher osteoporosis risk in a patient already with osteopenia. Raloxifene provides equivalent invasive cancer risk reduction (NSABP P-2) with fewer endometrial events and favorable bone effect.
